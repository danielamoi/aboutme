Research

My dissertation, Explaining the Adoption of Sustainable Consumption Practices, comprises three empirical studies conducted in Germany, Australia and the United Kingdom. Looking ahead, I want to move from stated intentions to observed, real-world behaviour — particularly in the food domain.

01

Sustainable and healthy diets

What drives people to adopt diets that are good for both the planet and their health? Extending the Comprehensive Action Determination Model, I compare the roles of environmental concern, social and personal norms, perceived consumer effectiveness, self-efficacy and health consciousness among German consumers.

Survey research · Structural equation modelling · Food Quality and Preference (2025)

02

Blended meat & alternative proteins

As a visiting researcher with the Low Harm Hedonism Initiative at the University of Queensland, I studied how omnivores perceive blended meat products (part meat, part plants) through in-depth interviews. Follow-up work tests menu-label disclosure experimentally and in a tasting study with UQ's FoodLab — building on my master's research on plant-based, insect-based and cultured meat.

Qualitative interviews · Online & tasting experiments · Australia

03

Reduced consumption & upward social comparison

When people compare themselves to someone who consumes less, are they more willing to refrain from purchases? I test this experimentally, examining green consumption values and temperance character strengths as moderators — including in the context of Christmas gift-giving in the UK.

Experiments · Moderation & mediation · Social comparison theory

04

Food, wellbeing & mental health

Resilient by Recipe (with Karina Konieczny) explores whether a dietary lunch intervention can support entrepreneurs' mental health, combining founder interviews with wearable-based physiological measurement. The intervention study is planned for 2027.

Field intervention · Wearables · Validated psychological scales
